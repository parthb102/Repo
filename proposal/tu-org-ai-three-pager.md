Together, these frame **two mutually reinforcing tracks**: a **value track** — prioritizing and transforming the domains where AI will drive outsized value — and an **organization track** — building the structure, workforce, and culture to enable and sustain it. ## Page 2 — The questions we would answer

Framed DD-style: the questions the program must answer, not the activities it runs.

### Track 1 — AI domain transformation & prioritization (value track)

1. **Where is the value?** Which domains hold material value at stake — anchored in TU's strategic priorities, and concentrated where fragmented data, high exception rates, and judgment-intensive decisions have kept prior automation from reaching it?
2. **How does each domain operate today** — the operating and cost baseline, at the depth needed to size AI potential and compare domains?
3. **What is each domain's AI impact potential** — revenue growth, cost reduction, cost avoidance, risk reduction — weighed against its strategic readiness and feasibility?
4. **Which 2–3 domains are TU's big bets — and which are the no-regret moves to start now?**
5. **In what sequence should TU execute** — with what owners, milestones, and enabling investments?

### Track 2 — Organization of the future

**Org hygiene**
1. What does a right-sized TU look like — optimal spans, layers, and leadership density (leader-to-teams ratio)?
2. Where does today's structure deviate most from benchmark — and what is the value at stake in closing the gap?

**Platform org of the future**
3. Which archetypes do true platform companies follow — central platform division (e.g., Atlassian, Salesforce), federated platform services (e.g., AWS) — and which is right for TU?
4. What are the core design choices — US vs. international P&L, regional vs. global layers, where technologists and platform product managers sit — and **what would TU have to believe** to make each?

### Platform-org archetypes — research brief (for the deep-dive page)

Archetypes that genuine thick-platform / thin-product companies follow. The GM model appears only as the contrast — the "from," not the "to" (per Megha: Google and Amazon retail follow GM models and are not platform orgs).

**A. Central platform division — "platform as a product"**
One platform organization with its own leadership, roadmap, funding, and platform product managers; product units are thin and consume the platform through internal contracts.
- Atlassian: dedicated Platform division after its cloud migration — identity, editor, data platform shared by Jira, Confluence, and the rest
- Salesforce: the core Salesforce Platform underpinning the Sales / Service / Marketing Clouds
- Microsoft: platform assets (Azure, Graph, identity) as first-class divisions with product experiences on top
- What TU must believe: platform can be funded centrally without being captured by the loudest product team; internal consumers will behave like customers; platform-PM talent can be hired or grown
- Fit signal: the closest match to TU's stated "thick platform, thin products" ambition

**B. Federated platform services — service-owner model**
No single platform division; the platform is a mesh of independently owned services behind hard API contracts, each with a single-threaded owner; products compose services.
- AWS: hundreds of service teams, "you build it, you run it"
- What TU must believe: elite API and ops discipline across the org; tolerance for duplication at the edges
- Fit signal: usually a later-stage destination — demanding for a company still standardizing its platform

**C. GM-led with shared platform underneath — the contrast archetype**
Business GMs own product P&Ls end to end (product + engineering + go-to-market); a central function provides shared technology, but businesses can and do build around it.
- Amazon retail businesses, YouTube within Google — Megha's own examples of what platform orgs are *not*
- Why it matters for TU: this is the drift risk of the current functional/regional structure — platform fragmentation with every P&L optimizing locally

**Design axes that cut across archetypes** (these become the "choices and what you must believe" page): platform as P&L vs. cost center vs. internally charged; who owns the platform roadmap (CTO vs. platform president vs. product council); thin product P&Ls vs. one enterprise P&L; central platform funding vs. consumption-based; US vs. international P&L and regional layers (AZ's live question); where platform PMs and technologists sit.

**Future-ready workforce (strategic workforce planning in the AI era)**
5. How will AI and automation change TU's talent demand over the next 3–5 years?
6. What is the future workforce's size, shape, and skill composition — which roles are **reshaped, replaced, or net-new**, and which skills are built vs. bought?
7. What is the **value case** for the workforce transition — and the roadmap and 90-day mobilization plan to capture it?

**Global location strategy**
8. What location footprint best serves the future workforce (GLS 2.0) — which roles sit where, and what moves now vs. later?

**Culture**
9. Structure alone does not change outcomes: what mindset and behavior shifts does the new organization demand — and does TU have the **change capacity** to absorb them?

## Page 3 — Building blocks and indicative timeline (~3-month program)

Lego blocks with a timeline on top — not a work plan. Tracks can run staggered (org starting ~week 3) or fully in parallel.

### Track 1 — AI domain transformation & prioritization (weeks 1–8)

| Block | Timing | What happens |
|---|---|---|
| Identify high-value domains | Wk 1–2 | Complete the initiative inventory already underway; cluster into end-to-end domains — **first read of the domain map ready for the board session** |
| Establish detailed baselines | Wk 2–4 | Operating and cost baseline for each candidate domain |
| Align the prioritization framework | Wk 3–4 | Criteria and weightings: value, readiness, feasibility |
| Assess impact, readiness, feasibility | Wk 4–6 | Score every domain; size the value at stake |
| Rank and decide | Wk 6–7 | Heatmap scorecard; leadership prioritization workshop — select 2–3 priority domains |
| Value-backed execution roadmap | Wk 7–8 | Sequence execution — owners, milestones, enabling investments |

→ *Exits into 12–14-week lighthouse initiatives in the priority domains — scaling only what demonstrates measurable impact*

### Track 2 — Organization of the future (weeks 3–12, indicative)

| Block | Timing | What happens |
|---|---|---|
| Current-state blueprint + hygiene diagnostic | Wk 3–5 | Org, FTE, and cost baseline; spans, layers, and leadership density vs. benchmark |
| Platform org design | Wk 5–8 | Archetypes and design choices; target structure for the platform era |
| Strategic workforce plan in the AI era | Wk 6–10 | Talent supply and demand with AI/automation potential; roles reshaped / replaced / net-new |
| Location footprint (GLS 2.0 refresh) | Wk 8–11 | Disposition future roles to target locations; what moves now vs. later |
| Culture, change, and talent roadmap | Wk 10–12 | Mindset and behavior shifts and change-capacity plan; phased implementation roadmap and 90-day mobilization |

*(Optional visual: a thin culture-and-change band spanning weeks 6–12 so all five fronts are visible on the page.)*

### Required callout — the interlock (bottom of page)

> **The two tracks feed each other.** As priority domains are transformed, teams are reconfigured bottom-up — new role mixes, team shapes, and skills. Those learnings flow back into the top-down strategic workforce plan; the refreshed plan, in turn, steers where talent, skills, and locations flex next — a continuous rhythm of learning, investment, and reallocation between the two.
