## Page 1 — Context for this discussion

**Four short paragraphs (Megha's flipped order — value track first):**

1. TransUnion is embarking on a journey to **identify and prioritize 2–3 enterprise domains for AI-led reimagination**. Leadership has begun inventorying AI initiatives across the enterprise; the next step is to cluster them into end-to-end domains, size the value at stake, and focus investment where it can fundamentally reshape the economics of the business — the small number of cross-functional domains that typically hold 40–50% of enterprise value

2. This comes at an **inflection point**. TU has evolved from credit bureau to product company and is now becoming a **platform organization — thin products built on thick platforms** — while still early in its AI journey. The organization's structure has not kept pace: in leadership's own words, TU is *"in its global-platforms era, with a structure from the 2012 era"*

3. Against that backdrop, there is an **organizational imperative spanning five fronts**:
   1. **Org hygiene** — right-sizing spans, layers, and leadership density (leader-to-non-leader ratios)
   2. **The platform org of the future** — a structure that matches how TU now builds and delivers
   3. **A future-ready workforce for the AI era** — with the AI-ready skills to power it
   4. **Global location strategy and footprint**
   5. **Culture** — the mindset and behavior shifts to make the new organization work

4. Together, these frame **two mutually reinforcing tracks**: a **value track** — prioritizing and transforming the domains where AI will drive outsized value — and an **organization track** — building the structure, workforce, and culture to enable and sustain it. Today's discussion: align on the questions each track must answer, and the shape of a ~3-month program to answer them

*Optional fifth beat (echoes JM's comment, use if VG wants a "why now" edge): "The risk today is not ambition but diffusion — many pilots, no air-traffic control; activity on GenAI without a clear line to value."*

## Page 2 — The questions we would answer

Framed DD-style: the questions the program must answer, not the activities it runs.

### Track 1 — AI domain prioritization (value track)

1. **Where is the value?** Which domains hold material value at stake, anchored in TU's strategic priorities and value pools?
2. **How does each domain operate today** — process flows, people, unit cost, and performance?
3. **What criteria should govern prioritization** — and how should value, readiness, and feasibility be weighted?
4. **What is each domain's AI impact potential** (revenue growth, cost reduction, cost avoidance, risk reduction) — and its strategic readiness and feasibility?
5. **Which domains are the big bets, and which are the no-regret moves** — how do they rank-order?
6. **In what sequence should TU execute** — with what owners, milestones, and enabling investments?

### Track 2 — Organization of the future

**Org hygiene**
1. What does a right-sized TU look like — optimal spans, layers, and **leader-to-non-leader ratios**?
2. Where does today's structure deviate most from benchmark — and what is the value at stake in closing the gap?

**Platform org of the future**
3. What platform-org archetypes should TU learn from — **GM-led** (e.g., Amazon businesses, YouTube), **functionally-led** (e.g., Apple), or **thick-platform / thin-product** (e.g., Salesforce, Adobe, Stripe, Atlassian) — and which model fits TU?
4. What are the core design choices — platform vs. product P&L ownership, global platform vs. regional layers, where technologists and platform product managers sit — and **what would TU have to believe** to make each?

**Future-ready workforce (strategic workforce planning in the AI era)**
5. How will AI and automation change TU's talent demand over the next 3–5 years?
6. What is the future workforce's size, shape, and skill composition — which roles are **reshaped, replaced, or net-new**?
7. Which AI-ready skills must be built at scale — and how fast can they be built vs. bought?

**Global location strategy**
8. What location footprint best serves the future workforce (GLS 2.0) — which roles sit where, and what moves now vs. later?

**Culture**
9. What mindset and behavior shifts does the new organization demand — and does TU have the **change capacity** to absorb them?

## Page 3 — Building blocks and indicative timeline (~3-month program)

Lego blocks with a timeline on top — not a work plan. Tracks can run staggered (org starting ~week 3) or fully in parallel.

### Track 1 — AI domain prioritization and value roadmap (weeks 1–8)

| Block | Timing | What happens |
|---|---|---|
| Identify high-value domains | Wk 1–2 | Complete the initiative inventory already underway; cluster into end-to-end domains |
| Baseline and framework | Wk 2–4 | Build each domain's operating baseline; align prioritization criteria and weightings |
| Assess and rank | Wk 4–6 | Score impact, readiness, and feasibility; build the heatmap scorecard |
| Prioritize | Wk 6–7 | CM1 prioritization workshop; select 2–3 priority domains |
| Value-backed roadmap | Wk 7–8 | Sequence execution — owners, milestones, enabling investments |

→ *Exits into domain transformation waves: 12–14-week proofs of value in the priority domains, scaling only what demonstrates measurable impact*

### Track 2 — Organization of the future (weeks 3–12, indicative)

| Block | Timing | What happens |
|---|---|---|
| Current-state blueprint + hygiene diagnostic | Wk 3–5 | Org, FTE, and cost baseline; spans, layers, and leadership density vs. benchmark |
| Platform org design | Wk 5–8 | Archetypes and design choices; target structure for the platform era |
| Strategic workforce plan in the AI era | Wk 6–10 | Talent supply and demand with AI/automation potential; roles reshaped / replaced / net-new |
| Location footprint (GLS 2.0 refresh) | Wk 8–11 | Disposition future roles to target locations; what moves now vs. later |
| Talent and culture roadmap | Wk 10–12 | Phased implementation roadmap, 90-day mobilization plan, change and culture plan |

### Required callout — the interlock (bottom of page)

> **The two tracks feed each other.** As priority domains are transformed, teams are reconfigured bottom-up — new role mixes, team shapes, and skills. Those learnings flow back into the top-down strategic workforce plan; the refreshed plan, in turn, steers where talent, skills, and locations flex next. Top-down and bottom-up iterate continuously — the workforce plan is a living plan, not a one-time exercise.
